LadderSort: Adaptive Sorting via Interleaved Nondecreasing Subsequences

Published — IEEE Access (Oct 2026)
M. M. H. Nayab, R. N. B. Rais, O. Khalid, and I. A. Khan. IEEE Access, vol. 14, pp. 170095–170113, Oct. 2026 (COMSATS University Islamabad & Ajman University; Advisor: Dr. Osman Khalid).
  • Formulated a deterministic, comparison-based sorting algorithm parameterized by $K$, the minimum number of nondecreasing subsequences covering the input; proved online decomposition optimality $K_{\text{LS}}(A) = K^*(A) = \text{LDS}(A)$ via Dilworth's theorem, $O(N \log(K+1))$ time, $O(N+K)$ auxiliary space, and $\Theta(N \log\sqrt{N})$ average-case complexity.
  • Engineered a two-phase C++20 implementation combining a three-stage stateful hinted search (local probe, bidirectional exponential gallop, binary search) over a contiguous tail vector with a $K$-dispatched merge (direct return for $K=1$, galloping two-way merge for $K=2$, flat-array loser tree for $K>2$) and a hybrid $\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil$ fallback guardrail for high-$K$ adversarial inputs.
  • Benchmarked against TimSort, IntroSort (std::sort), QuickSort, PatienceSort, and EncroachingListsSort on Apple M1 up to $N = 10^8$: achieved up to 1.45× wall-clock speedup over TimSort on a 55,364-row GH Archive event trace ($K=368$), 1.38×–2.66× speedups over subsequence-adaptive baselines, and 2–4 comparisons per element on small-$K$ workloads.

Dialect-Level Generalization of Arabic Sentiment Classifiers: An Exact-Duplicate-Free, Uncertainty-Aware Evaluation of Encoders and LLMs

Preprint (CC BY 4.0) — Nov 2026
M. M. H. Nayab, S. G. Raza, and B. Bibi. Preprint (CC BY 4.0), Nov. 2026.
  • Audited ArSarcasm and ArSarcasm-v2 using text normalization, exact character 4-gram Jaccard near-duplicate detection, and MADAR-6/NADI dialect-ID cross-checks, identifying 44 leaked train–test duplicates in the official ArSarcasm release and curating leak-free splits over 5,089 unique binary tweets across MSA, Egyptian, Levantine, and Gulf Arabic.
  • Benchmarked 11 fine-tuned transformer encoders (MARBERTv2, AraBERTv02, CAMeLBERT variants, XLM-R-Base) and 4 instruction-tuned 7–9B LLMs (Fanar-1-9B, Qwen2.5-7B, AceGPT-v2-8B, ALLaM-7B) across pooled, size-matched leave-one-dialect-out (LODO), and MSA-only regimes over 5 seeds, replicating cross-dialect transfer findings on ArSarcasm-v2.
  • Built an uncertainty-aware evaluation suite combining 2,000-resample hierarchical bootstrap intervals, TOST equivalence testing ($\pm 0.02$), Holm–Bonferroni corrected McNemar tests, and a NumPyro Bayesian beta-binomial mixed model (NUTS); demonstrated that variance-based indices (DRI) rank a majority classifier #1 of 79 systems on F1, establishing level-aware HM-F1 (MARBERTv2: 0.870; Fanar-1-9B 8-shot: 0.849) and WD-F1 instead.

A Reproducible Benchmark for Roman Urdu Emotion Classification via LLM-Based Dataset Construction and Orthographic Variation Modeling

Preprint (CC BY 4.0) — 2026
M. M. H. Nayab and G. Batool. Preprint (CC BY 4.0), 2026.
  • Executed an idempotent 54-run evaluation grid across 27 configurations on dual NVIDIA T4 GPUs sweeping orthographic variation $k \in \{0,1,3\}$ and epochs $\in \{2,3,4\}$ across XLM-R, mBERT, and MuRIL, obtaining 0.2909 canonical 28-class Macro-F1 (3-seed mean $0.2894 \pm 0.0025$) and 0.5004 Ekman-6 Macro-F1 (70.31% accuracy) with XLM-R.
  • Established via a size-controlled ablation that orthographic augmentation gains vanish at fixed training volume, and audited experimental artifacts to document MuRIL optimization collapse in 3/9 grid cells and a $\approx 0.01$ Macro-F1 single-example perturbation noise floor.
