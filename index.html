About Me

Malik Mir Hamza Nayab is a computer science researcher and software engineer who completed his Bachelor of Science in Computer Science from COMSATS University Islamabad, Abbottabad Campus (CGPA 3.72/4.00, Spring 2026) and was recognized as Salutatorian at Edwardes College Peshawar. His research spans adaptive and parallel algorithms, algorithm engineering, low-resource and multilingual NLP, cross-dialect robustness, uncertainty-aware statistical evaluation, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).

As first author, he developed LadderSort, a deterministic adaptive sorting algorithm published in IEEE Access (vol. 14, pp. 170095–170113, Oct. 2026) that dynamically adapts to the minimum number of nondecreasing subsequences $K$, proving online decomposition optimality $K_{\text{LS}}(A) = K^*(A) = \text{LDS}(A)$ via Dilworth's theorem and achieving up to 1.45× wall-clock speedup over TimSort on real-world GitHub Archive workloads. In NLP, he audited Arabic sarcasm benchmarks for train–test data leakage and demonstrated that variance-based indices like DRI rank a majority-class baseline #1, establishing level-aware HM-F1 and WD-F1 metrics alongside Bayesian and bootstrap statistical evaluation.

In addition, Hamza published the 54,263-sample Roman Urdu GoEmotions benchmark dataset on Zenodo and completed his undergraduate thesis on a bilingual (English/Roman Urdu) mental-health RAG dialogue system. In engineering, he solely architected and deployed MiddelMen (middelmen.com), a production social-commerce trust platform. He also served as Interim Lead & Co-Lead for Google Developer Groups on Campus (GDGoC) at COMSATS Abbottabad.

LadderSort: Adaptive Sorting via Interleaved Nondecreasing Subsequences

Published • IEEE Access
First Author — IEEE Access, Vol. 14, pp. 170095–170113, Oct. 2026
  • Formulated a deterministic, comparison-based C++20 sorting algorithm parameterized by $K$ (minimum nondecreasing subsequences), proving online decomposition optimality $K_{\text{LS}}(A) = K^*(A) = \text{LDS}(A)$ via Dilworth's theorem.
  • Achieved up to 1.45× wall-clock speedup over TimSort on real-world GH Archive traces ($N=55,364, K=368$) and 1.38×–2.66× speedups over subsequence-adaptive baselines on Apple M1 up to $N = 10^8$.

Dialect-Level Generalization of Arabic Sentiment Classifiers: An Exact-Duplicate-Free, Uncertainty-Aware Evaluation of Encoders and LLMs

Preprint • Nov 2026
First Author — M. M. H. Nayab, S. G. Raza, and B. Bibi. Preprint (CC BY 4.0), Nov. 2026
  • Audited ArSarcasm and ArSarcasm-v2 benchmarks for train–test data leakage (identifying 44 leaked pairs) and curated leak-free evaluation splits over 5,089 unique binary tweets across MSA, Egyptian, Levantine, and Gulf Arabic.
  • Benchmarked 11 transformer encoders (MARBERTv2, AraBERTv02, CAMeLBERT) and 4 instruction-tuned 7–9B LLMs (Fanar-1-9B, Qwen2.5-7B) across pooled, LODO, and MSA-only transfer regimes over 5 seeds.
  • Demonstrated that variance-based indices (DRI) rank a majority baseline #1 of 79 systems, establishing level-aware HM-F1 and WD-F1 metrics alongside a NumPyro Bayesian beta-binomial mixed model (NUTS) and bootstrap evaluation suite.
